Which paper supports which claim?

Full citations, exact versions, and what was actually read.

Start with Jørgensen–Weichwald–Hammond for collective agency, Hammond and coauthors for causal reasoning in games, Lauffer and coauthors for strategic knowledge, and Ehrlich and coauthors for neural representations of feedback. The contrasting accounts below help identify what each formalization leaves unresolved.

Shared BibTeX · Structured reading guide · Checked illustrative results

Closest prior work: mechanized causal abstraction and behavioral collective agency.

Frederik Hytting Jørgensen, Sebastian Weichwald, and Lewis Hammond. (2026). Causal Foundations of Collective Agency. arXiv:2605.00248v1, 30 April 2026; CLeaR 2026.

Read: PDF pp. 1–13 of 31

Use and limit: The abstraction framework, actor–critic result and voting application already exist; our comparison is a proposal.

Object-level versus mechanism-level interventions; pre- and post-policy queries.

Lewis Hammond, James Fox, Tom Everitt, Ryan Carey, Alessandro Abate, and Michael Wooldridge. (2023). Reasoning about causality in games. Artificial Intelligence, 320, 103919. DOI: 10.1016/j.artint.2023.103919.

Read: PDF pp. 1, 3, 10–12, 15–17 of 53

Use and limit: A change available to policy selection can induce adaptation. Multiple rational outcomes need sets or a selection rule.

Strategic equivalence and the information needed to recover a best-response set.

Niklas Lauffer, Ameesh Shah, Micah Carroll, Michael D. Dennis, and Stuart Russell. (2023). Who Needs to Know? Minimal Knowledge for Optimal Coordination. Proceedings of the 40th International Conference on Machine Learning, PMLR 202, 18599–18613.

Read: PDF pp. 1–7 of 15

Use and limit: Equal full best-response sets are stronger than one shared optimal action. Computational development focuses on common-payoff games; the paper states the theory extends to general-sum games.

Feedback factorization, observation masking, and reported neural belief-patching experiments.

David A. Ehrlich, Manuel Baltieri, Fernando E. Rosas, and Alexander B. Boyd. (2026). Beliefs in feedback: Factored representations of interacting agents in transformers. arXiv:2611.02047v1, 1 November 2026.

Read: PDF pp. 1–10, 24–25, 34–35 of 38

Use and limit: Product-prior and timing assumptions matter. Some sns-containing settings fail to steer. Synthetic prediction evidence does not establish agency or human control.

Counterfactual adaptation and the role of learning/design in the agent boundary.

Zachary Kenton, Ramana Kumar, Sebastian Farquhar, Jonathan Richens, Matt MacDermott, and Tom Everitt. (2022). Discovering Agents. arXiv:2208.08345v2, 24 August 2022.

Read: PDF pp. 1–6 of 34

Use and limit: Use the version actually read. The discovery theorems and their assumptions have not been fully audited here.

A contrasting autonomous account: regulation and norms grounded in organization.

Xabier E. Barandiaran, Ezequiel Di Paolo, and Marieke Rohde. (2009). Defining Agency: Individuality, Normativity, Asymmetry, and Spatio-temporality in Action. Adaptive Behavior, 17(5), 367–386. DOI: 10.1177/1059712309343819.

Read: PDF pp. 1, 5–8 of 20

Use and limit: Goal-directed behavior under an external payoff does not by itself meet this account.

Mutual maintenance of constraints across timescales.

Maël Montévil and Matteo Mossio. (2015). Biological organisation as closure of constraints. Journal of Theoretical Biology, 372, 179–191. DOI: 10.1016/j.jtbi.2015.02.029.

Read: PDF pp. 1, 8–13 of 23

Use and limit: This organizational closure differs from predictive informational closure. Reading uses the 23-page author manuscript dated 9 March 2016.

Information assessed by causal contribution to a specified viability measure.

Artemy Kolchinsky and David H. Wolpert. (2018). Semantic information, autonomous agency and non-equilibrium statistical physics. Interface Focus, 8, 20180041. DOI: 10.1098/rsfs.2018.0041.

Read: PDF pp. 1–3 of 17

Use and limit: Only the opening framework and examples were read; later formal results are not used. Viability and scrambling interventions require specification.

Robust representational and goal states as a contrasting account of group agency.

Christian List and Philip Pettit. (2006). Group Agency and Supervenience. The Southern Journal of Philosophy, 44(S1), 85–105. DOI: 10.1111/j.2041-6962.2006.tb00032.x.

Read: PDF pp. 1–4 of 22

Use and limit: Reading uses the author manuscript revised 11 January 2006. Aggregation impossibility proofs were not read.

Tracking relevant human reasons; tracing to human understanding and responsibility.

Filippo Santoni de Sio and Jeroen van den Hoven. (2018). Meaningful Human Control over Autonomous Systems: A Philosophical Account. Frontiers in Robotics and AI, 5, 15. DOI: 10.3389/frobt.2018.00015.

Read: PDF pp. 1, 7–9 of 14

Use and limit: Proposed necessary conditions, not a validated scale or evidence that islands outperform alternatives.

Maximum-entropy goal-directedness as a candidate quantitative comparator.

Matt MacDermott, James Fox, Francesco Belardinelli, and Tom Everitt. (2024). Measuring Goal-Directedness. Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems, 37.

Read: PDF pp. 1–5 of 20

Use and limit: Utility and causal-model choices remain explicit. Appendix proofs and experiments unread.

Predictive causal states and closure.

Fernando E. Rosas, Bernhard C. Geiger, Andrea I. Luppi, Anil K. Seth, Daniel Polani, Michael Gastpar, and Pedro A. M. Mediano. (2024). Software in the natural world: A computational approach to hierarchical emergence. arXiv:2402.09090v2, 5 June 2024.

Read: Cumulative PDF pp. 1–10, 14–30 of 33 from earlier review.

Use and limit: Closure concerns specified variables and futures; a constant macro can pass. No unrestricted intervention guarantee.

Minimal directional sufficient statistics across a chosen bipartition.

Fernando E. Rosas, Pedro A. M. Mediano, Martin Biehl, Shamil Chandaria, and Daniel Polani. (2020). Causal blankets: Theory and algorithmic framework. arXiv:2008.12568v2, 29 September 2020.

Read: All 12 PDF pages previously read.

Use and limit: Blanket existence does not select an agent; whole-trajectory decoupling has additional assumptions.

Action-conditioned interfaces and world-model presentations.

Fernando E. Rosas, Alexander Boyd, and Manuel Baltieri. (2025). AI in a vat: Fundamental limits of efficient world modelling for agent sandboxing and interpretability. arXiv:2504.04608v1, 6 April 2025.

Read: PDF pp. 1–14 of 38 from earlier review; version verified by exact checksum.

Use and limit: Do not replace a controlled action law with observational conditioning under feedback; a sufficient belief need not be minimal.

Background on modular world-model descriptions.

Alexander Boyd, Franz Nowak, David Hyland, Manuel Baltieri, and Fernando E. Rosas. (2026). From monoliths to modules: Decomposing transducers for efficient world modelling. arXiv:2512.02193v2, 20 May 2026; initially submitted 2025.

Read: PDF pp. 1–25 of 36 from earlier review.

Use and limit: Formal feedforward setting; no general empirical result about all feedback networks. Further reading, not the central agency criterion.

The authors’ distinction between epistemic and ontological blanket interpretations.

Jelle Bruineberg, Krzysztof Dołęga, Joe Dewhurst, and Manuel Baltieri. (2022). The Emperor’s New Markov Blankets. Behavioral and Brain Sciences, 45, e183. DOI: 10.1017/S0140525X21002351.

Read: Target-article PDF pp. 1–3 of a 76-page treatment. Commentaries and response unread.

Use and limit: Used as an interpretive caution, not a comprehensive assessment of active inference or a refutation of causal blankets.

Motivation for testing AI islands.

XOR Labs. (2026). The Dimensional Balance of Power. Supplied HTML working draft, 26 October 2026; associated with Alec’s presentation.

Read: Six substantive sections read in the earlier review.

Use and limit: No verified public URL or individual author byline. Our workflow details are an operationalization. No empirical superiority claim.

Background on reveal order, lookback, and reconstruction.

Simon Schrader. (n.d.). Coupled Transducers and Reveal Orders: Setup. Supplied 14-page working draft.

Read: All 14 PDF pages read in the earlier review.

Use and limit: Proposition 3(b) and approximation experiments are explicitly unchecked in the supplied draft. Not used as an established result.

Two elementary diagnostics used in the revised essays and companions.

Sandy Tanwisuth. (2026). Exact illustrations of strategic reports, objective changes, and action rights. Repository-authored Python script, checked 18 November 2026.

Read: All five deterministic partitions checked using exact rational arithmetic.

Use and limit: No novelty or empirical-effect claim. Results depend on the explicit invented payoff table and uniform prior.

Research provenance and limits

The private meeting conversation and original attachments informed the initial questions. They are not publicly hosted. This revision made a targeted search for the requested Hammond, Lauffer, and feedback papers and contrasting behavioral, autonomous, and institutional accounts of agency. This is not a systematic or exhaustive review. Source records retain exact versions, checksums, selected-page coverage, and failed retrievals.

The proposed empirical contribution is a comparison of predictive compression, strategic abstraction, and correction capacity. Novelty across all neighboring literatures has not been established. No neural replication or organizational pilot has been run.
